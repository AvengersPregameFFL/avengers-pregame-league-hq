THE JUGS KEEP ROLLING

Some teams make the playoffs. Some win championships. And once in a generation, a team comes along that makes everyone else question why they paid $50 to participate.

Ladies and gentlemen, the Juggernauts have arrived.

A league-record 209.50 points. Six consecutive victories. A staggering 65–12 all-play record. That's an 84.4% winning percentage through 11 weeks.

And they did it without first-round draft pick Jaxon Smith-Njigba. Jordan Love and Matthew Golden were on bye, Josh Jacobs was injured, Davante Adams was on IR, and Dalton Kincaid was sidelined with an injury.

Dak Prescott dropped 43.90. CeeDee Lamb added 30.20. Ashton Jeanty delivered 27.90.

Apparently, the Jugs can rest their best weapons and still commit statistical war crimes.

You'd think making league history would earn a little congratulations. Maybe even some support from the people you consider friends.

Instead, success has a funny way of revealing just how many miserable bastards you've surrounded yourself with.

“What did ya win?”

Nothing yet, Mike. That's how records work. You make history, they write your name down, and people remember it. I understand why this is unfamiliar territory.

Then came Alyssa, declaring the Jugs had won “biggest f**.”

Spoken like a true loser. That's why I busted your ass this week.

Equality for everyone. Accountability for everyone else.

Not a single congratulations. Just bitterness, jealousy, and unsolicited opinions from the cheap seats.

They say it's lonely at the top.

Because everyone you know is looking up while I'm looking down on you bitches.

THE ART OF WINNING WITH WHAT YOU'VE GOT

And here's the part that should terrify the rest of the league: this wasn't even the Jugs at full strength.

The bench was a graveyard of bye weeks and injuries. Most managers would've spent the week preparing excuses.

Instead, the Jugs went shopping.

Deebo Samuel? Off waivers. Chicago Bears defense? Off waivers. Dak Prescott? Off waivers.

That last one cost $50 in FAAB—because when a quarterback can win you a week, you don't stand around waiting for permission.

The Jugs outbid Alyssa for Dak, then watched him drop 43.90 points in the very matchup against her.

She didn't just lose the game. She lost the bidding war for the guy who helped beat her.

That's a two-part humiliation.

And don't overlook Cam Little. His last name may be Little, but the difference he's making every week is anything but. Even the kicker is putting belt to ass.

This wasn't luck. This was adapting, overcoming, and making the moves other managers wish they'd made.

The starters showed up. The waiver wire delivered. The whole bench was on bye or injured.
