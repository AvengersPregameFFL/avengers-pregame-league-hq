EXHIBIT A: THE SOFTEST DEFENSE IS HIS SCHEDULE

Nick has faced just 606.74 total points in five weeks—fewest in the league. That's 121.35 per game, compared with roughly 145.40 points scored per team per week across the league. His opponents have scored about 24 points per week below the league average. That's not a tiny scheduling break; that's a weekly head start.

EXHIBIT B: THREE LAST-PLACE OPPONENTS

In Week 1, Aaron scored 120.16, the week's lowest total. In Week 2, Travis scored 82.52, dead last. In Week 5, Jake scored 91.40, also last. Three of Nick's five opponents were the lowest-scoring team in the entire league that week. It's hard to lose when your opponent keeps bringing a pool noodle to a sword fight.

EXHIBIT C: THE WEEK 5 ESCAPE

Nick put up 101.76 against Jake's 91.40. Nick was seventh of eight in weekly scoring; he would've lost to six other teams that week. Instead, he got the one opponent he could beat. That's the kind of matchup fortune that makes statisticians spit out their coffee.

EXHIBIT D: HOW GOOD IS HE REALLY?

Nick has scored 783.54 points, third-most in the league. His 24–11 all-play record translates to a 68.6% win rate against every possible weekly opponent. Strong? Absolutely. Invincible? Not remotely. Across five weeks, his all-play results are equivalent to approximately 3.43 wins in five randomly matched games, not five. That's a descriptive comparison—not a prediction or a guarantee.

THE EXPERT VERDICT

League HQ's fictional analytics desk says: REGRESSION WATCH. Nick has a legitimate top-three scoring offense, so calling him a fraud would be bad analysis. But his 5–0 record overstates how dominant he's been, and the unusually low points-against total is unlikely to remain such a reliable safety net. If his opponents start scoring near the league average, his margin for error shrinks fast. The evidence supports expecting some losses eventually—not a certain collapse or a guaranteed playoff exit.

The schedule has been carrying Nick like a make-a-wish quarterback. Sooner or later, somebody's going to make him actually earn one.
